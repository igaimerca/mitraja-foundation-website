Apollo Program

Building spaces for young people to learn, participate and lead. The Apollo Program builds on the legacy of Apollo Club and creates opportunities for young people to develop skills, confidence, relationships and leadership.

Summer Camp

Learning beyond the classroom. Summer Camp brings together children, youth and community members through a mix of learning, creativity, health, sports and exploration.

More importantly, it demonstrates what can happen when community members contribute not only participation, but also time, knowledge, resources and leadership.

Community Ownership

Funded by the community, for the community

Community contribution represented 45.74% of Summer Camp's total budget, through cash and in-kind contributions from Mitraja members and the local community. Local resource persons facilitated for free, and some even contributed money themselves.

This is one of our strongest learnings, and it will guide how we plan every future program: start with what a community already has (trust, volunteer effort, shared ownership) before reaching for outside funding.

Learning Out Loud

What we're carrying into the next program

We keep our reflections visible on purpose. A foundation that only shows its wins isn't being honest about how community work actually gets built.

Challenges we saw

  • • Limited women's participation in some activities: only 2 women joined two of the sessions.
  • • Publicity and information about the camp didn't reach the whole community.
  • • Fundraising lacked a clear plan going in. We need a budget before the next one starts.

What we're changing

  • • Define facilitator roles clearly, with a registration process ahead of each session.
  • • Assign a real-time note-taker to capture participant reflections, missing this time.
  • • Map community strength and resource people before, not during, a program.
  • • Map and document local & medicinal plants in the local language, our next Green Skills step.

What's Next: A Two-Year Initiative

Roots & Belonging

Sidhabari's Ward 1 and 2 are home to around 400 households from five communities, Hajong, Dalu, Garo, Bengali Hindu and others, resettled here after the 1964 East Pakistan riots displaced tens of thousands of people into the Matia refugee camp system. Roots & Belonging is our proposed two-year programme to work with that shared history directly, building on everything above into a structured, community-governed initiative around identity, livelihoods and representation.
