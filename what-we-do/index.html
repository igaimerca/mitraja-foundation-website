What We Do

Recognition. Resources. Representation.

Mitraja works with displaced communities to strengthen the systems that enable people to shape their own futures. We use three interconnected pillars.

01 · Recognition

Know what already exists

Every community carries knowledge: stories, histories, skills, culture, ecology, memory, experience. But what is not recognised can easily be forgotten, undervalued or disconnected from opportunity. Mitraja works with communities to document, revive and communicate what already exists.

History Documentation

Recording community histories and stories.

Culture Revival

Supporting communities to reconnect with cultural practices and traditions.

Traditional Knowledge Conservation

Recognising local ecological and community knowledge systems.

Community Knowledge

Identifying knowledge-keepers and creating spaces for intergenerational learning.

Communication & Learning

Building the ability of young people and communities to communicate their stories and ideas.

02 · Resources

Connect potential with opportunity

Recognition is only the beginning. Communities also need access to resources, skills, knowledge, networks and opportunities. Mitraja works to connect community potential with these resources, through creative livelihoods, learning resources like Apollo Library, and partnerships with organisations, practitioners and institutions.

Crochet · Knitting · Embroidery · Bamboo Craft · Handloom · Pottery

The objective is not simply to teach a skill. It is to explore how local knowledge and creativity can become pathways to meaningful and sustainable livelihoods.

Learning Resources

Apollo Library

Through initiatives such as Apollo Library, we help create community-owned spaces where people can access books, knowledge, workshops and learning opportunities. Built from bamboo and donated books by community volunteers, in collaboration with the Free Library Network, CML (an initiative of Tata Trust) and Snehjori. Two community volunteers travelled to a Free Library Network seminar in Guwahati (their first night away from the village) and came back to take ownership of library activities, unprompted.

Apollo Club

Apollo Club is our hub for learning and resources: a community-owned space that empowers youth to build confidence, create opportunities, and become changemakers within their own communities, by connecting education, entrepreneurship, local knowledge and community action.

Our Exit Plan

The measure of success is not dependence

Our work is designed with an exit in mind. As community leadership and decision-making structures become stronger, activities can increasingly be planned and managed locally. Community committees become capable of carrying forward initiatives. Local resource people become knowledge-keepers. Young people become leaders. And Mitraja gradually steps back.

Our goal is simple: build something that can continue without us.

What's Next: A Two-Year Initiative

Roots & Belonging

Sidhabari is home to around 400 households from five communities, Hajong, Dalu, Garo, Bengali Hindu and others, resettled here in Refugee Camp No. 8 and Camp No. 9 after the 1964 East Pakistan riots displaced tens of thousands of people into the Matia refugee camp system. That shared history of displacement is still the quietest, least-addressed layer of what this community carries. Roots & Belonging is our proposed two-year programme to work with it directly, not just livelihoods and skills, but identity, confidence and belonging.

Roots & Recognition

Community history documentation, intergenerational dialogue and cultural revival, addressing the identity and confidence gap underneath the material poverty.

Roots & Resources

Creative livelihoods training, a dedicated women's economic pathway, and support accessing the land rights and entitlements many households are legally owed.

Roots & Representation

A community governance model the community designed itself: regular all-gender meetings, local sub-committees, and a recognised network of local facilitators.
